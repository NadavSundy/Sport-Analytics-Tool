## Task — `PUB-01`

**Task given to participant:**

> You are interested in analysing cricket data.
>
> Starting from the application, find a fixture that interests you and show the
> information available about it.

### Outcome

- [x] Success
- [ ] Partial
- [ ] Failure

### Observations

- **Navigation/approach taken:** Started at the home page. Read past the opening
  statement and a diagram before reaching a section headed "Fixtures — Open
  published matches", which told her that a fixture is a match. Used "Browse
  fixtures" from there. Selected England v Australia, 13 June 2005 — the second
  entry in the list — on the grounds that she had heard of both countries.
- **Confusion/hesitation:** The home page did not tell her what to do. It states
  "T20 CRICKET ANALYTICS" and that the game is measured ball by ball. Below that
  is a diagram with no labels and an example card reading "Delivery 7.3",
  "runs.offBat = 4" and "Three values move", none of which meant anything to her.
  She only learned what a fixture was from a later section.
- **Errors encountered:** The fixtures list opened partway down the page, at an
  unrelated match, so she scrolled up to find the start. The match page did the
  same, opening at the result rather than the top.
- **Assistance requested:** None.
- **Assistance given:** None.
- **Participant comments:** *"OK, England won, but I don't know what half of
  these words are."*
- **Positive observations:** She reached a fixture and read the outcome
  correctly, unaided, with no prior knowledge of the sport. She independently
  worked out that 179 minus 79 is 100 and therefore that runs behave like points.

Terms she could not interpret: "T20", "SEASON 2005" (she asked why a year is a
season), "MATCH TYPE", "TEAM TYPE", "BALLS PER OVER", "SCHEDULED OVERS", "SUPER
OVERS INCLUDED", "Innings", "DELIVERY RUNS", "PENALTY RUNS", "Based on 125
accepted events", "Complete data", "PUBLISHED MATCH RECORD", "View calculation
trace", and "Day Match" on the theme control.

The page displayed "Competition name unavailable" where a competition should be,
and "Weather is unavailable for this fixture's venue." The venue itself is never
named anywhere on the page.

### Findings from this task

| Finding ID | Finding | Severity | Initial decision |
| ---------- | ------- | -------- | ---------------- |
| F01 | The home page does not establish what the product is or what to do first. Its explanatory example uses raw field syntax ("runs.offBat = 4", "Three values move") and an unlabelled diagram. | S3 | Pending |
| F02 | List and detail pages open partway down rather than at the top, so the participant scrolled up twice to find where a page began. | S3 | Pending |
| F03 | "Competition name unavailable" is presented as a fact on the fixture page. | S3 | Pending |
| F04 | The venue is never stated. The only reference to it is a message reporting that weather is unavailable for it. | S3 | Pending |
| F05 | Domain and interface vocabulary is presented without any explanation, and a participant with no cricket background could not interpret most of the page. | S2 | Pending |

F05 is the same underlying issue as P01's F07, but it had a materially larger
effect here. For P01 the unexplained terms were an irritation; for P02 they were
the reason PUB-02 could not be completed.

## Task — `PUB-02`

**Task given to participant:**

> Choose a fixture and find useful statistics about its players or teams.
>
> Explain what you believe the information is showing you.

### Outcome

- [ ] Success
- [x] Partial
- [ ] Failure

### Observations

- **Navigation/approach taken:** Stayed on fixture 8936. Selected the player with
  the largest number she had seen. Followed his name, then returned and opened
  his calculation trace instead. Also visited the England team page.
- **Confusion/hesitation:** She could identify which numbers were large and
  which player they belonged to, but could not say with confidence what most of
  them measured.
- **Errors encountered:** Following a player's name led to that player's
  cross-competition match history, beginning with a 2018 county fixture. She
  asked why he was playing for a different team. The return link on that page
  reads "Back to players" rather than returning to the match, so she used the
  browser's Back button; the match page then reopened at the top and she had to
  scroll down again to find the same player.
- **Assistance requested:** None.
- **Assistance given:** None.
- **Participant comments:** *"I can see who scored the most, but I'm guessing at
  most of these labels."*
- **Positive observations:** She reasoned correctly about several figures without
  being told anything. She derived overs from legal balls divided by balls per
  over, derived economy rate from runs conceded divided by overs, and worked out
  that an "accepted event" is a delivery the player was involved in by adding
  balls faced to balls bowled and matching the stated total.

Her recorded interpretations, with her own confidence:

| Label | Her reading | Confidence |
| ----- | ----------- | ---------- |
| RUNS 46 | Points scored for his team | Fairly sure |
| BALLS FACED 26 | How many times the ball came at him | Fairly sure |
| STRIKE RATE 176.92 | How often he hit it; cannot be a percentage above 100 | Not sure |
| FOURS 5 / SIXES 2 | "He came 4th five times? Or scored 4 points five times?" | Not sure |
| RUNS CONCEDED 8 | Points given away to the other team | Somewhat sure |
| LEGAL BALLS 12 | Balls he threw that were allowed, so some are not | Guessing |
| OVERS 2.0 | 12 divided by 6 | Fairly sure |
| ECONOMY RATE 4 | "Money?" then, on seeing 8 ÷ 2, points given away per over | Guessing |
| WICKETS 2 | "Two of something good?" | No idea |
| "Based on 38 accepted events" | 26 + 12, so an event is a ball he was part of | Fairly sure, after adding up |

Three things she flagged as apparent errors:

1. A bowler showing 15 legal balls and "OVERS 2.3". She calculated 15 ÷ 6 as 2.5
   and concluded 2.3 was wrong. **It is correct** — 2.3 is two overs and three
   balls in standard cricket notation. This is a labelling problem, not a
   calculation error.
2. The England batters' runs sum to 173 while the innings total reads 179. Six
   runs are unaccounted for on the page and nothing explains where they came
   from.
3. One player's card reads "Based on 1 accepted event", which she read as that
   player having touched the ball once.

The England team page contains a list of matches and a list of players, with no
statistics about the team.

### Findings from this task

| Finding ID | Finding | Severity | Initial decision |
| ---------- | ------- | -------- | ---------------- |
| F06 | Player cards are not grouped by team and the two sides are interleaved, so one team's players cannot be read together. | S3 | Pending |
| F07 | A player's name links to their cross-competition match history rather than to their part in the current match, and the return link goes to the players list rather than back to the fixture. | S3 | Pending |
| F08 | Returning to a fixture by browser Back loses the reading position. | S4 | Pending |
| F09 | A player's calculation trace begins at an unexplained delivery number, for example "Delivery 73". | S3 | Pending |
| F10 | Statistical labels — strike rate, economy rate, wickets, fours, sixes, legal balls — are presented with no explanation of what they measure. | S2 | Pending |
| F11 | Over notation such as "2.3" is read as a decimal and appears to contradict the legal-ball count beside it. The figure is correct; the presentation is not self-explanatory. | S3 | Pending |
| F12 | The batters' runs do not sum to the innings total and nothing on the page accounts for the difference. | S2 | Pending |
| F13 | "Based on N accepted events" is uninterpretable without reconstructing the arithmetic. | S3 | Pending |
| F14 | Team pages carry no team-level statistics. | S3 | Pending |

F12 requires investigation. The difference is consistent with extras, which the
innings total includes and the player cards do not, but no extras figure appears
on the page. Whether the correct resolution is to display extras or to explain
the difference is a design decision.

## Task — `PUB-04`

**Task given to participant:**

> You want to continue analysing the fixture data using another tool.
>
> Find a way to obtain the relevant event data for use outside the application.

### Outcome

- [ ] Success
- [x] Partial
- [ ] Failure

### Observations

- **Navigation/approach taken:** Found the panel headed "Export this trace —
  Download the currently displayed accepted-event slice for analysis" on the
  England innings page and clicked "Download CSV" because it was the first
  button.
- **Confusion/hesitation:** She did not know what CSV or JSON are and the page
  does not explain either. After clicking, nothing changed on screen and the
  button looked the same, so she was unsure whether anything had happened. She
  did not know which program would open the file.
- **Errors encountered:** No confirmation of the download was given.
- **Assistance requested:** None.
- **Assistance given:** None.
- **Participant comments:** *"I clicked it… did it do anything?"* and, on opening
  the file, *"I don't know who any of these people are. It's just numbers."*
- **Positive observations:** She located the export control unaided.

The participant obtained the file but could not determine whether the action had
succeeded, what the file was, or what to do with it. The outcome is Partial on
that basis.

**Method note.** The examination of the downloaded file took place after the
session, at the facilitator's request, and the analysis of its contents below was
performed by the facilitator. It is recorded here because it bears directly on
this task, but it was not observed behaviour during the session.

The file she downloaded contains 100 rows. The page she took it from displayed
125 events and a total of 179 runs. Summing the runs column in the file gives
132; the last row is delivery 15.6. The final 25 deliveries and 47 runs are
absent, with no indication in the file or on the page.

Every identity in the file is an opaque numeric identifier. No player, team or
competition name appears. Several columns are empty on almost every row, one
column reads "false" throughout, and the only word she recognised was "caught" on
a small number of rows.

### Findings from this task

| Finding ID | Finding | Severity | Initial decision |
| ---------- | ------- | -------- | ---------------- |
| F17 | The export silently returns at most 100 events regardless of how many the page displays. The participant's file totals 132 runs against the 179 shown on screen. | S1 | **Accept** |
| F18 | No confirmation is given that a download has occurred. | S3 | Pending |
| F19 | The export control's wording is entirely technical — "trace", "accepted-event slice", "CSV", "JSON" — and none of it is explained. | S3 | Pending |
| F20 | The export contains no participant, team or competition names. | S2 | **Accept** |
| F21 | Columns that do not apply export as empty cells rather than zero, which reads as missing data. | S4 | **Accept** |
| F22 | A column reads "false" on every row without explanation. | S3 | Pending |

F17 was verified by the facilitator and raised as issue #467. The events endpoint
pages at 50 by default and rejects a limit above 100; the export issues a single
request at the maximum page size and discards the pagination cursor, while the
page itself follows it.

F20 and F21 were verified and raised as issue #468.

F22 is the same control P01 described as "Non-boundary: No". It is the
`runsNonBoundary` flag, which is `false` on effectively every delivery. The value
appears correct; the problem is displaying it at all to a reader who cannot
interpret it.
